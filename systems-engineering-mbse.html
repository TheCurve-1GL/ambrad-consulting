Systems engineering

Systems engineering and MBSE for infrastructure asset owners

An owner keeps a bridge, a tunnel or a network for fifty years or more. Systems engineering gives you a clear line from what the asset must do, through how it was designed and built, to how it is inspected and maintained. We set it up so the owner, not the project, holds that line.

Why it matters

Projects finish, assets don't

On most schemes the requirements, interface registers and design models belong to the delivery team. When the contract ends they are archived, and the owner is left with drawings and spreadsheets that don't explain why the asset was designed the way it was.

Model-based systems engineering (MBSE) replaces that pile of documents with one connected model. Done from the owner's side, it means that when you change, renew or extend an asset, you can see straight away what else it affects.

Questions

Common questions

What is systems engineering in infrastructure?

A way of making sure a complex asset does what it's meant to do. It starts with clear requirements, manages the interfaces between parts, and checks at each stage that the design and the built asset still meet the need.

What does MBSE add?

Model-based systems engineering holds the requirements, functions, interfaces and physical parts in one linked model instead of separate documents. A change in one place shows its effect everywhere else, which makes renewals and upgrades far easier to plan.

Is this only for big new projects?

No. Owners get most value on existing assets, when planning a renewal, an upgrade or a change of use. That's when knowing why the asset was designed a certain way matters most.

Do we need to buy MBSE software first?

No. We start with the people, the process and who will own the model. The tool comes after, once you know what you need it to do.
