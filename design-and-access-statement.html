Design and Access Statements for planning applications

A Design and Access Statement explains how the proposal responds to its site and how people will get to and use it. We write it around your architect's drawings, so the story the officer reads matches the scheme they see.

Why it matters

The document that ties the application together

A Design and Access Statement is required for major development, for listed building consent, and in conservation areas and World Heritage Sites for one or more new homes or 100 square metres or more of new floorspace.

It's often the first document a planning officer reads. A good one sets out the context, the principle of the development and the design choices in a logical order, and points to the technical reports that back it up.

Design and Access Statement

What the statement covers

01

Site and context

The location, the existing building, the surrounding character and uses, and how accessible the site is.

02

Heritage

Listed buildings and conservation areas nearby, their significance, and the effect of the proposal on them.

03

The proposal

Use, amount, layout, scale, appearance and landscaping, and the principle of the development against local and national policy.

04

Access

Inclusive design, the approach and entrance, getting around inside, accessible rooms or homes, parking, servicing and emergency access.

How it works

From brief to issue-ready report

The same four steps for every document, whether it's one report or a full set for the application.

Brief

You send the site address, the drawings and a description of the scheme. We check what your council's validation list asks for.

Research

We gather the Environment Agency, council and site information the document needs, and flag anything that could hold the application up.

Report

You receive signed, issue-ready documents for submission, and we answer questions from the planning officer or consultees.

Questions

Common questions

When is a Design and Access Statement required?

For major development, for listed building consent, and in conservation areas and World Heritage Sites for one or more new homes or 100 square metres or more of new floorspace. Your council may also ask for one on other schemes.

How long should it be?

Proportionate to the scheme. A small change of use needs a short, focused statement. A larger scheme needs more. We keep it as short as the application allows.

Do you work with my architect?

Yes. We use the architect's drawings and design thinking, and we agree the draft with them before it's issued.

Does it cover heritage?

Yes, we set out the heritage context, its significance and the effect of the proposal. For a listed building, a separate specialist Heritage Statement may also be needed, and we'll tell you early if it is.
