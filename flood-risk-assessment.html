Why it matters

When the flood report holds up the application

If your site sits in Flood Zone 2 or 3, is larger than one hectare, or has a known surface water problem, your council will expect a site-specific Flood Risk Assessment. Change of use counts too: turning an office into homes or a hotel can make the building more vulnerable to flooding, and the report has to show it will be safe.

A thin or generic report is one of the most common reasons an application is held up or refused. We assess the site properly, set out what the design needs, if anything, and make the case in plain terms the planning officer can follow.

Flood Risk Assessment

What the assessment covers

01

All sources of flooding

Rivers, the sea, surface water, groundwater, sewers, reservoirs and the flood history of the site, using Environment Agency data and the council's Strategic Flood Risk Assessment.

02

Climate change

The current Environment Agency allowances for peak river flow, rainfall intensity and sea level rise, applied to your site and the lifetime of the development.

03

Sequential and Exception Tests

Whether the tests apply to your scheme, and if they do, the evidence that it passes them. Some changes of use are exempt from the Sequential Test, and we say so.

04

Making the development safe

Finished floor levels, flood resistance and resilience, safe access and escape, a flood warning and evacuation plan, and the effect on flood risk elsewhere.

05

Surface water drainage and SuDS

How rainwater is managed now and after development: run-off rates, storage, sustainable drainage (SuDS) such as green roofs, permeable paving and rain gardens, and who maintains it. Included in the FRA, or as a separate drainage strategy if your council wants one.

Questions

Common questions

When do I need a Flood Risk Assessment?

In England, you normally need a site-specific Flood Risk Assessment for development in Flood Zone 2 or 3, for sites of one hectare or more in Flood Zone 1, and for sites at risk from other sources such as surface water. A change of use to a more vulnerable use, such as office to homes or a hotel, can trigger one too. Your council's validation list has the final say.

How do I find out which flood zone my site is in?

Search your address on the Environment Agency's Flood Map for Planning. It shows Flood Zones 1, 2 and 3 for rivers and the sea. Surface water risk is shown separately, so we check both.

What are the Sequential and Exception Tests?

The Sequential Test asks whether the development could go somewhere at lower flood risk. The Exception Test, where it applies, asks the scheme to show wider benefits and that it will be safe for its lifetime. The FRA sets out which rules apply to your site.

Do you prepare drainage strategies too?

Yes. For many schemes the surface water drainage strategy sits as a chapter of the FRA. Where your council or the Lead Local Flood Authority wants a separate report, we provide one, following the drainage hierarchy and the current climate change allowance on rainfall.

Do I need a site visit?

Not always. Most of the research is desk-based using Environment Agency and council data. We visit when levels, access routes or the building itself need checking on the ground.

What do you need from me to start?

The site address, existing and proposed drawings, a short description of the development, and any pre-application advice from the council. We'll tell you if anything else is needed.
