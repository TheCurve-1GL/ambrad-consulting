The problem

The documents that hold up a planning application

If your site sits in Flood Zone 2 or 3, is larger than one hectare, or has a known surface water problem, your council will expect a site-specific Flood Risk Assessment. Change of use counts too: turning an office into homes or a hotel can make the building more vulnerable to flooding, and the report has to show it will be safe.

The flood report rarely travels alone. The same application usually needs a Design and Access Statement, a sustainability or energy statement and often a transport document. We can prepare them together, so the facts, drawings and commitments match across every document.

Flood risk and planning support

Documents we prepare

01

Flood Risk Assessment

Every source of flooding (rivers, the sea, surface water, groundwater, sewers and reservoirs), climate change allowances, the Sequential and Exception Tests, floor levels, flood resistance and resilience, safe access and a flood warning and evacuation plan.

02

Surface water drainage strategy

How rainwater leaves the site now and after development, with sustainable drainage (SuDS) and any chances to reduce run-off. Included in the FRA or as its own report.

03

Design and Access Statement

The site, its context and heritage, the proposal, and how the design and access arrangements meet local and national policy.

04

Sustainability and Energy Statement

The energy strategy, carbon emissions, overheating, water use, materials and waste, with BREEAM where your council asks for it.

05

Travel Plan

How guests, residents or staff will reach the site, the measures that encourage walking, cycling and public transport, and how the plan will be monitored.

Questions

Common questions

When do I need a Flood Risk Assessment?

In England, you normally need a site-specific Flood Risk Assessment for development in Flood Zone 2 or 3, for sites of one hectare or more in Flood Zone 1, and for sites at risk from other sources such as surface water. A change of use to a more vulnerable use, such as office to homes or a hotel, can trigger one too. Your council's validation list has the final say.

What does a Flood Risk Assessment include?

It identifies every source of flooding to the site, applies climate change allowances, deals with the Sequential and Exception Tests, and sets out how the development will be safe: floor levels, flood resistance and resilience, safe access, flood warning and evacuation, and surface water drainage.

What are the Sequential and Exception Tests?

The Sequential Test asks whether the development could go somewhere at lower flood risk. The Exception Test, where it applies, asks the scheme to show wider benefits and that it will be safe for its lifetime. Some changes of use are exempt from the Sequential Test, and the FRA sets out which rules apply to your site.

Can you prepare the other planning documents too?

Yes. We prepare Design and Access Statements, Sustainability and Energy Statements and Travel Plans, either on their own or together with the Flood Risk Assessment, so every document tells the same story.

What do you need from me to start?

The site address, existing and proposed drawings, a short description of the development, and any pre-application advice from the council. We'll tell you if anything else is needed.

Where do you work?

Across England. Most of the research is desk-based using Environment Agency and council data, and we visit the site where the assessment needs it.
