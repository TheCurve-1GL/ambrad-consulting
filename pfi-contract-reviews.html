Why it matters

Whoever finds the problem first sets the price

Hundreds of UK PFI contracts reach the end of their term over the next fifteen years. At handback, the assets must meet the condition the contract requires, and disputes are won by the side with the better evidence.

During the contract, money moves the same way: deductions are decided by the payment mechanism and the records behind it. We've worked on operational PFI for the private side, then both, then the public side, so we know what each side will look for.

What the review covers

01

Handback readiness

The gap between today's asset condition and the handback requirements, what it would cost to close, and a realistic programme to close it before expiry.

02

Deductions and performance exposure

Where availability and performance deductions are likely under your payment mechanism, and what they'd cost if nothing changes. Common on highways DBFO contracts.

03

The Client's-Eye Review

An independent look at your contract, assets and records from your counterparty's point of view, for contractors, SPVs or authorities.

04

Expiry and transition planning

The asset information, records, systems and people needed at handover, whether the service is retendered or brought in-house.

Questions

Common questions

What is PFI handback?

At the end of a PFI or PPP contract, the assets return to the public sector client in the condition the contract requires. If they fall short, the contractor can face deductions, retentions or claims. Preparing early gives time to close the gap at a sensible cost.

When should we start preparing for expiry?

Government guidance recommends starting at least seven years before the contract ends. The earlier the condition gap is known, the more options both sides have.

Do you work for authorities or contractors?

Both. We've worked on the private side, the public side and both at once, so we understand what each side needs from the evidence.

What is a deductions exposure review?

A look at your availability and performance regime to find where deductions are likely and what they would cost. It turns a list of risks into a number the board can act on.
