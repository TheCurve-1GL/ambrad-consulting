Why it matters

Many policies, one statement

Sustainability policy is spread across the local plan, and in London across the London Plan too. Energy, overheating, water, materials, waste, air quality and drainage are often checked by different officers.

We pull it into one statement with a clear structure, so each officer finds their section quickly and the commitments match the rest of the application. Where a refurbishment re-uses an existing building, we make that case properly, because it's often the strongest sustainability argument the scheme has.

Sustainability and Energy Statement

What the statement covers

01

Energy strategy and carbon

The energy hierarchy (Be Lean, Be Clean, Be Green, Be Seen), baseline and proposed carbon emissions, and any carbon offset, coordinated with the energy assessor's SBEM or SAP calculations.

02

Overheating and cooling

How the design avoids overheating, following the cooling hierarchy before any active cooling.

03

BREEAM

The route to the target rating where your council asks for one, worked out with the BREEAM assessor.

04

Water, materials and waste

Water efficiency, responsible materials, the circular economy, and construction and operational waste.

05

Pollution and amenity

Air quality, noise, light pollution, refrigerants, construction impacts and land contamination, at the level the scheme needs.

How it works

From brief to issue-ready report

The same four steps for every document, whether it's one report or a full set for the application.

Brief

You send the site address, the drawings and a description of the scheme. We check what your council's validation list asks for.

Research

We gather the Environment Agency, council and site information the document needs, and flag anything that could hold the application up.

Report

We write the document, set out anything the design needs to change, and agree a draft with you and your architect.

Issue

You receive signed, issue-ready documents for submission, and we answer questions from the planning officer or consultees.

Questions

Common questions

What do Be Lean, Be Clean, Be Green and Be Seen mean?

They're the four steps of the London Plan energy hierarchy: reduce energy demand first, supply energy efficiently, use renewable energy on site, then monitor and report real performance after completion. Many councils outside London use a similar approach.

Do you carry out the SBEM, SAP or BREEAM assessments?

Those calculations and the formal BREEAM assessment are done by accredited assessors. We work alongside them, and our statement brings their results, the design and the policy together.

Does my scheme need BREEAM?

It depends on your council. Many set a BREEAM target for non-residential schemes above a certain size. We'll check your council's policy at the start.

Can it cover drainage and flood risk too?

Yes. The statement summarises flood risk and sustainable drainage and points to the Flood Risk Assessment, so the two documents agree.
