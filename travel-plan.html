Planning support

Travel Plans for planning applications

A Travel Plan shows how residents, guests or staff will reach the site, and what the development will do to encourage walking, cycling and public transport. We keep it practical, measurable and in line with your council's guidance.

Why it matters

Show the council how people will get there

Councils ask for a Travel Plan when a development will generate a noticeable amount of travel, or where parking is limited and the scheme relies on sustainable travel. In London, Transport for London guidance shapes what's expected.

A good Travel Plan is short, realistic and specific to the site. It sets targets the occupier can meet, names who is responsible, and explains how progress will be checked.

Travel Plan

What the plan covers

01

Site accessibility

Public transport, walking and cycling routes, and the public transport accessibility level (PTAL) in London.

02

Trip estimates

Indicative trips by each mode, based on stated assumptions and comparable sites.

03

Objectives and targets

Clear, measurable targets for how people will travel, set at a level the occupier can actually meet.

04

Measures

Cycle parking, travel information for residents or guests, delivery and servicing arrangements, and car-free or car-light commitments where they apply.

05

Management and monitoring

A named travel plan coordinator, travel surveys and a review timetable agreed with the council.

Questions

Common questions

When is a Travel Plan needed?

Councils set their own thresholds, usually based on the size and use of the development. Many ask for one on hotels, larger residential schemes and workplaces. Your council's validation list or pre-application advice will say.

What is PTAL?

The public transport accessibility level, used in London to rate how well a site is served by public transport, from 0 (worst) to 6b (best). A high PTAL supports car-free development.

Is a Travel Plan the same as a Transport Assessment?

No. A Transport Assessment or Transport Statement assesses the transport impact of a development. A Travel Plan sets out how travel will be managed once it's occupied. If your scheme needs a full Transport Assessment, we'll tell you early.

Can you keep it consistent with the other documents?

Yes. When we prepare several documents for the same application, the travel information in the Design and Access Statement and the Travel Plan match.
