Why clients call us

Applications stall on the supporting documents.

A planning application is only as strong as the reports behind it. A missing Flood Risk Assessment can stop it being validated. A generic one invites objections. Documents written by different people often disagree on floor levels, numbers of rooms or parking, and the officer notices.

We prepare the technical documents together, from one set of drawings and facts, and write them so the officer can follow the argument quickly. Where the design needs to change to pass a test, we say so early, while it's still cheap to change.

How it works

From brief to issue-ready report

You see the findings before the report is issued, so there are no surprises at submission.

Brief

You send the site address, the drawings and a description of the scheme. We check what your council's validation list asks for.

Research

We gather the Environment Agency, council and site information the document needs, and flag anything that could hold the application up.

Report

We write the document, set out anything the design needs to change, and agree a draft with you and your architect.

Issue

You receive signed, issue-ready documents for submission, and we answer questions from the planning officer or consultees.

How we work

Questions

Common questions

Which documents will my planning application need?

It depends on the site, the scheme and your council's validation list. Send us the address and a short description, and we'll tell you which documents are likely to be needed before you commit to anything.

Can you prepare all the documents for one application?

Yes. We prepare the Flood Risk Assessment, drainage strategy, Design and Access Statement, Sustainability and Energy Statement and Travel Plan, so the facts, drawings and commitments match across every document.

Who do you work with?

Developers, architects, planning consultants and owners, on schemes from single buildings and changes of use to larger developments, across England.

Who signs the reports?

Every report is prepared or checked by a chartered civil engineer (CEng MICE) with more than 28 years in UK infrastructure, who started out designing flood schemes.
