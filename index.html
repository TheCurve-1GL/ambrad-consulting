Why clients call us

Every department works. The joins between them often don't.

Large companies are usually run by many capable teams, systems and suppliers. Each one does its own job well. The trouble starts in the hand-offs that nobody owns: data copied from one system to another, a contract requirement that never reaches the maintenance plan, a report that takes a week because three teams hold a piece of it.

That's where the hours go, where the cost builds up and where the risk hides until a client, a regulator or a failure finds it first. We give you line of sight of those connections, so effort goes where it counts.

Asset management and contracts

The Client's-Eye Review

See what your client will find, in pounds, before they find it.

  • Deductions exposure. For highways operators and contractors: where performance deductions are likely, and what they'd cost.
  • Handback readiness. For PFI and PPP contracts nearing expiry: the condition gap and its price, while there's still time to act.

  • Maintenance spend in the right places. Hard numbers that help the board say yes to the right work, and stop the wrong work.

Approach

How an engagement runs

The same four stages, whether the work is a single process, a department or a whole network. You see the evidence at each stage before you commit to the next.

Diagnose

Map the work as it really happens, across teams, systems and suppliers. Find the joins where time, cost and risk leak out.

Design

Agree the changes, the owner of each one and the measure that will show it worked. Price each change in hours and pounds.

Improve

Make the changes in a small, measured pilot first. Fix what the pilot shows, then roll out with the people who do the work.

Sustain

Hand over the process models, measures and training so the improvement lasts after we've gone. You own everything we produce.

The three-way sort

Sort the work before you buy the tool

Most AI projects start with a product. Ours start with the work. Every regular task goes into one of three boxes, and only then do we talk about tools.

Keep it human
Judgement, relationships and accountability. Some work should never be automated, and we'll say so plainly.
AI drafts, a person checks
Reports, letters, first-pass analysis. AI does the heavy lifting and a named person signs it off.
AI handles it
Copying, matching, chasing and routine summaries. Repetitive work moves somewhere cheaper and more reliable.

Operations is the weak link in digital twins

In a 2024 UK survey, 73% of operations respondents said digital twin benefits were delivered "not well" or "poorly", and fewer than 9% of infrastructure respondents found the handed-over twin suited to asset management. The gap sits between delivery and operations.

Source: Ambrad research, November 2026.
