Why clients call us

Engineering judgement, written down clearly.

A planning application is only as strong as the reports behind it. A missing Flood Risk Assessment can stop it being validated, and documents written by different people often disagree on floor levels, rooms or parking. We prepare them together, from one set of facts, so the officer can follow the argument quickly.

Owners and operators face the same problem at a larger scale: good engineers and plenty of data, but scattered across teams and contracts. We help join it up, from the asset strategy down to the maintenance regime, and price the gaps so you know where to start.

About

A chartered civil engineer behind every piece of work

I started my career designing flood schemes. I still bring the same engineering care to every report and review.

Questions

Common questions

Which documents will my planning application need?

It depends on the site, the scheme and your council's validation list. Send us the address and a short description, and we'll tell you which documents are likely to be needed before you commit to anything.

Can you prepare all the documents for one application?

Yes. We prepare the Flood Risk Assessment and drainage strategy, the Design and Access Statement and the Sustainability and Energy Statement, and we've written Travel Plans for the same applications, so the facts, drawings and commitments match across every document.

Who do you work with?

For planning: developers, architects, planning consultants and owners across England. For asset and contract work: infrastructure owners, operators, O&M contractors and PFI project companies in highways, rail, airports, water and energy.

Do you work on PFI and PPP contracts?

Yes. We've worked on operational PFI for more than 15 years, on the private side, then both, then the public side, including handback, deductions and expiry planning.

Who signs the reports?

Every report is prepared or checked by a chartered civil engineer (CEng MICE) with more than 28 years in UK infrastructure, who started out designing flood schemes.
